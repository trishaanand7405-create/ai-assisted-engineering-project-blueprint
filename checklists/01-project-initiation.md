# Project Initiation Checklist

> **Use this checklist before substantial project work begins.**

The purpose of this checklist is to ensure that the team has enough clarity to begin work without creating avoidable confusion, rework or unmanaged risk.

---

## 1. Project Purpose

* [ ] The problem to be addressed is clearly stated.
* [ ] The intended users or stakeholders are identified.
* [ ] The expected product value is understood.
* [ ] The project objective is documented.
* [ ] The initial scope is understood.
* [ ] Important exclusions are identified.

---

## 2. Product Direction

* [ ] A Product Vision exists.
* [ ] Product Goals are defined.
* [ ] Initial User Stories are available.
* [ ] The initial Product Backlog has been established.
* [ ] Major assumptions are visible.
* [ ] Important constraints are documented.

---

## 3. Team Readiness

* [ ] Team members are identified.
* [ ] Responsibilities are understood.
* [ ] Communication channels are available.
* [ ] Required repositories or workspaces exist.
* [ ] The team understands the working model.
* [ ] Escalation or decision-making paths are understood.

---

## 4. Engineering Readiness

* [ ] The source-code repository exists.
* [ ] The basic project structure is established.
* [ ] Development environment requirements are documented.
* [ ] Required tools and runtimes are identified.
* [ ] Dependency-management approach is understood.
* [ ] Basic coding conventions are agreed.

---

## 5. AI-Assisted Engineering Readiness

* [ ] The team understands the role of AI in the project.
* [ ] AI-generated output will be reviewed before acceptance.
* [ ] Sensitive information will not be unnecessarily provided to AI systems.
* [ ] AI-assisted code will be tested.
* [ ] Important AI-generated decisions will remain subject to human judgement.
* [ ] The team understands that AI output is not automatically authoritative.

---

## 6. Quality Readiness

* [ ] Quality expectations are understood.
* [ ] Important requirements are testable.
* [ ] Acceptance Criteria can be defined.
* [ ] Initial testing strategy is understood.
* [ ] Important risks have been identified.
* [ ] Evidence requirements are understood.

---

## 7. Delivery Readiness

* [ ] The initial Sprint or delivery approach is understood.
* [ ] The first meaningful increment has been identified.
* [ ] Dependencies are known.
* [ ] Major blockers are identified.
* [ ] Definition of Done or equivalent completion criteria are understood.

---

## 8. Deployment and Maintenance Awareness

* [ ] The intended deployment environment is known.
* [ ] Basic deployment expectations are understood.
* [ ] Configuration requirements are identified.
* [ ] Recovery considerations are understood.
* [ ] Post-release maintenance responsibilities are recognised.

---

## 9. Final Initiation Check

Before substantial implementation begins, the team should be able to answer:

* [ ] What problem are we solving?
* [ ] Who are we solving it for?
* [ ] What value are we trying to create?
* [ ] What are we building first?
* [ ] How will we know that it works?
* [ ] How will AI assist us?
* [ ] What are the major risks?
* [ ] What is the next concrete action?

---

## Initiation Decision

### Ready

The team has sufficient clarity to begin.

### Not Ready

Important uncertainty remains.

Do not interpret "not ready" as project failure. Use the missing information to determine the next clarification or planning activity.

---

## Guiding Principle

> **Start with enough clarity to move forward, not with the illusion that everything must be known in advance.**
