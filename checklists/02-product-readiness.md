# Product Readiness Checklist

> **A product should be sufficiently understood before significant engineering effort is committed to building it.**

Use this checklist before moving from product discovery and definition into substantial implementation.

---

## 1. Problem Clarity

* [ ] The problem statement is documented.
* [ ] The problem represents a meaningful user or organisational need.
* [ ] The intended users are identified.
* [ ] Important stakeholders are known.
* [ ] The current situation or pain point is understood.
* [ ] Major assumptions are visible.
* [ ] The team can explain why the problem is worth solving.

---

## 2. Product Vision

* [ ] A Product Vision exists.
* [ ] The desired future state is clear.
* [ ] The product's intended value is understood.
* [ ] The vision is understandable to the whole team.
* [ ] The proposed solution is consistent with the problem being addressed.

---

## 3. Product Goals

* [ ] Product Goals are defined.
* [ ] Goals are connected to the Product Vision.
* [ ] Goals describe meaningful outcomes.
* [ ] Goals can be evaluated.
* [ ] Priorities between important goals are understood.
* [ ] The team knows which goals matter most for the current increment.

---

## 4. Users and Personas

* [ ] Primary users are identified.
* [ ] Relevant user personas are documented.
* [ ] Important user needs are understood.
* [ ] User workflows are sufficiently clear.
* [ ] Different user types and their differing needs are recognised.
* [ ] Assumptions about users are identified where evidence is limited.

---

## 5. User Stories

* [ ] Important user needs have been expressed as User Stories.
* [ ] Stories describe user value.
* [ ] Stories are understandable.
* [ ] Stories are appropriately sized for implementation.
* [ ] Acceptance Criteria can be defined.
* [ ] Dependencies and important assumptions are visible.

---

## 6. Product Backlog

* [ ] A Product Backlog exists.
* [ ] Backlog items are understandable.
* [ ] High-value items are identifiable.
* [ ] Priorities are established.
* [ ] Dependencies are visible where important.
* [ ] Large items can be decomposed when required.
* [ ] The backlog is expected to evolve as learning occurs.

---

## 7. Scope

* [ ] Initial product scope is documented.
* [ ] Out-of-scope items are understood.
* [ ] The Minimum Viable Product or initial increment is identified where appropriate.
* [ ] The team understands what does not need to be built yet.
* [ ] Scope is not being expanded simply because additional ideas are available.

---

## 8. Product Quality

* [ ] Important quality expectations are identified.
* [ ] Critical requirements are testable.
* [ ] Acceptance Criteria exist or can be established.
* [ ] Important usability considerations are understood.
* [ ] Security and privacy considerations are identified where relevant.
* [ ] Performance expectations are identified where relevant.

---

## 9. Product Risk

* [ ] Major product assumptions are visible.
* [ ] Important technical risks are recognised.
* [ ] Important user or adoption risks are recognised.
* [ ] Regulatory or organisational constraints are considered where applicable.
* [ ] High-risk assumptions have an appropriate validation approach.

---

## 10. AI-Assisted Product Work

* [ ] The team understands where AI may assist product work.
* [ ] AI-generated requirements or ideas will be reviewed.
* [ ] AI-generated assumptions will be validated.
* [ ] Sensitive user information will be handled appropriately.
* [ ] Human judgement remains responsible for product decisions.

---

## 11. Product Readiness Decision

Before substantial engineering begins, the team should be able to answer:

* [ ] What problem are we solving?
* [ ] Who are we solving it for?
* [ ] What value should the product create?
* [ ] What are we building first?
* [ ] What is explicitly out of scope?
* [ ] How will we know whether the product is successful?
* [ ] What assumptions still need validation?

### Ready

The product direction is sufficiently clear to begin or continue engineering.

### Not Ready

Important product uncertainty remains and should be addressed before committing significant implementation effort.

---

## Guiding Principle

> **Build only after the team has enough product clarity to know what it is trying to achieve and why.**
