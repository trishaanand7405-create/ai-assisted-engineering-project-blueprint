# Product Goals

> **The Product Vision describes where we want to go. Product Goals describe the outcomes we want to achieve.**

Product Goals translate the broad Product Vision into a small number of **clear, meaningful and achievable outcomes**.

---

## 1. What Is a Product Goal?

A Product Goal describes a **future state or capability** that the product should achieve.

A good Product Goal is:

* Clear
* Valuable
* Achievable
* Relevant to the Product Vision
* Understandable by the entire team
* Possible to evaluate

Avoid goals that simply describe technical tasks.

### Weak

> Build a machine-learning model.

### Better

> Enable users to identify high-risk cases from available data.

The second statement describes **product value**, not merely implementation.

---

## 2. Define the Primary Goal

Every project should have **one primary Product Goal**.

Complete:

> **Our Product Goal is to ______________________________.**

The goal should describe the most important outcome the team wants the product to deliver.

---

## 3. Supporting Goals

The team may define a small number of supporting goals.

Examples:

* Enable users to access meaningful insights.
* Reduce manual processing effort.
* Provide timely alerts.
* Improve visibility of important events.
* Enable simulation of selected scenarios.
* Support informed decision-making.

Keep the number of goals small.

> **A short list of meaningful goals creates focus.**

---

## 4. Goal → Evidence

A goal should have some form of evidence that helps the team determine whether progress has been made.

Ask:

> **How will we know that we are moving toward this goal?**

Possible evidence includes:

* Functional capability
* Measured performance
* Processing time
* Prediction quality
* Data quality
* User feedback
* Successful workflow execution
* Demonstrable simulation
* Successful deployment

Do not invent impressive metrics simply to make the project appear successful.

> **Measure what matters and what you can reasonably demonstrate.**

---

## 5. Goal Hierarchy

The relationship should remain clear:

``
PRODUCT VISION
       │
       ▼
PRIMARY PRODUCT GOAL
       │
       ├──────────────┐
       ▼              ▼
SUPPORTING GOAL   SUPPORTING GOAL
       │              │
       └──────┬───────┘
              ▼
        PRODUCT BACKLOG
              │
              ▼
          SPRINTS
```

Every significant backlog item should contribute to at least one Product Goal.

---

## Product Goal Quality Check

Before proceeding:

* [ ] Is there one clearly defined Primary Product Goal?
* [ ] Does it support the Product Vision?
* [ ] Does it describe an outcome rather than a technical task?
* [ ] Can the team explain why the goal matters?
* [ ] Is there reasonable evidence by which progress can be evaluated?
* [ ] Are supporting goals limited to what is genuinely necessary?

### Principle

> **Goals create focus. Evidence creates accountability.**

The Product Goal provides the bridge between **Product Vision** and the **User Stories and Product Backlog** that follow.
