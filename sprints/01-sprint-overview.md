# Sprint Overview

> **Eight short Sprints. One complete product journey.**

This workshop simulates a real-world Agile product development lifecycle.

Each team works on a product from **Product Vision through Deployment, Maintenance and Enhancement**.

The objective is not to produce a production-ready enterprise system in eight hours.

The objective is to **experience how a professional engineering team works**.

---

## 1. Workshop Sprint Model

The workshop provides:

* 10 Agile teams
* Approximately 6 students per team
* 8 Sprints
* Approximately 1 hour per Sprint
* 4 project tracks
* A common Product → Agile → Engineering workflow

Each team continuously evolves the same product throughout the Sprints.

---

## 2. The Eight-Sprint Journey

``
SPRINT 1
Product Discovery
      ↓
SPRINT 2
Product Definition
      ↓
SPRINT 3
Solution Design
      ↓
SPRINT 4
Implementation
      ↓
SPRINT 5
Integration & Testing
      ↓
SPRINT 6
Working Product
      ↓
SPRINT 7
Deployment & Release
      ↓
SPRINT 8
Maintenance & Enhancement
```

The exact activities are defined in the individual Sprint playbooks.

---

## 3. Every Sprint Produces Evidence

A Sprint should not end with only a discussion.

Each team should produce something observable, such as:

* Product decision
* Backlog update
* User Story
* Design
* Code
* Data
* Model
* Pipeline
* Agent workflow
* Simulation
* Test evidence
* Demonstration
* Deployment
* Enhancement

The evidence becomes progressively more concrete as the product evolves.

---

## 4. Sprint Rhythm

Each one-hour Sprint follows a simple rhythm:

``
PLAN
  ↓
EXECUTE
  ↓
INTEGRATE
  ↓
REVIEW
  ↓
RETROSPECT
```

The team should avoid spending the entire hour discussing what it intends to build.

> **Every Sprint should move the product forward.**

---

## 5. Continuous Product Evolution

The product does not restart at the beginning of every Sprint.

Instead:

``
SPRINT N
   ↓
WORKING INCREMENT
   ↓
FEEDBACK
   ↓
UPDATED BACKLOG
   ↓
SPRINT N+1
```

New information may change priorities, designs or implementation decisions.

This is an intentional part of Agile product development.

---

## 6. What Students Should Experience

By the end of Sprint 8, every team should have experienced:

* Product thinking
* Agile roles
* Backlog management
* Sprint planning
* Collaborative engineering
* Technical decision-making
* AI-assisted development
* Testing and validation
* Product demonstration
* Release thinking
* Deployment
* Maintenance
* Continuous improvement

### Core Learning Objective

> **Experience the complete journey from an idea to an evolving product — as an Agile engineering team.**
