# Sprint Structure

> **Every Sprint has a clear goal, a focused execution window and a visible outcome.**

The workshop uses **one-hour Sprints** to simulate the rhythm of professional Agile product development.

The time constraint is intentional.

Teams must learn to **prioritise, collaborate, make decisions and deliver value under time pressure**.

---

## 1. The One-Hour Sprint

Each Sprint follows the same basic structure:

``
00–10 min   PLAN
10–45 min   EXECUTE
45–55 min   REVIEW
55–60 min   RETROSPECT
```

The exact timing may be adjusted by the facilitator when necessary, but the basic rhythm should remain consistent.

---

## 2. PLAN — 10 Minutes

The team determines:

* Sprint Goal
* Stories / tasks to address
* Responsibilities
* Dependencies
* Expected outcome
* Definition of Done

The Product Owner helps clarify **what matters most**.

The Scrum Master keeps the team focused on the Sprint.

The Engineer(s) help determine **how the work can be achieved**.

> **Plan enough to start confidently — do not spend the Sprint planning the Sprint.**

---

## 3. EXECUTE — 35 Minutes

The team works on the selected Sprint items.

Activities may include:

* Product analysis
* Design
* Coding
* Data preparation
* Model development
* Pipeline construction
* Agent implementation
* Simulation
* Testing
* Integration
* Documentation

Team members should collaborate rather than work as six isolated individuals.

---

## 4. REVIEW — 10 Minutes

The team demonstrates what was actually achieved.

The review should answer:

* What did we complete?
* What is working?
* What evidence do we have?
* What changed?
* What remains incomplete?
* What did we learn?

A partially completed feature may be demonstrated if its current state is clearly explained.

> **Demonstrate reality, not intention.**

---

## 5. RETROSPECT — 5 Minutes

The team quickly reflects:

### What went well?

Identify something worth continuing.

### What did not go well?

Identify one important problem.

### What will we change?

Choose one concrete improvement for the next Sprint.

Avoid turning the Retrospective into a long discussion.

---

## 6. Sprint Output

Every Sprint should leave behind at least one visible outcome.

Examples:

``
Decision
Design
Backlog Update
Working Code
Data
Model
Pipeline
Agent Workflow
Simulation
Test Evidence
Release
Enhancement
```

The outcome depends on the Sprint.

---

## 7. Time Discipline

The one-hour constraint is part of the learning experience.

Teams should:

* Prioritise aggressively.
* Keep discussions focused.
* Avoid unnecessary perfection.
* Ask for clarification quickly.
* Integrate early.
* Demonstrate what actually works.

If something cannot be completed, **make the unfinished state visible and move forward**.

---

## Sprint Principle

> **Plan briefly. Build deliberately. Demonstrate honestly. Learn quickly.**

Eight short Sprints should produce a **continuous product journey**, not eight disconnected classroom exercises.
