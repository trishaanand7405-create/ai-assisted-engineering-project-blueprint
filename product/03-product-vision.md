# Product Vision

> **A Product Vision describes the future we want to create through the product.**

The Product Vision connects the **problem, users and desired value** into one clear direction for the team.

It should be understandable to a user, stakeholder, engineer or instructor without requiring technical detail.

---

## 1. What the Vision Should Answer

A good Product Vision answers:

* **Who** are we serving?
* **What problem** are we addressing?
* **What are we creating?**
* **What value** will it provide?
* **What better future** are we trying to enable?

The Vision should describe the destination, not the implementation details.

---

## 2. Vision Statement

Use this structure as a starting point:

> **For [target users] who [experience a problem], our product will [provide a capability] that [delivers value], unlike [current approach], because [key value or advantage].**

The team may adapt the wording.

The structure is more important than the exact sentence.

---

## 3. What to Avoid

A Product Vision should **not** become a technology specification.

Avoid statements such as:

> "We will build a Python application using TensorFlow and a REST API."

That describes implementation.

Instead:

> "We will provide users with faster and more reliable access to actionable insights from their operational data."

Technology decisions belong in the engineering process.

---

## 4. Vision and Technology

The four project tracks may use very different technologies:

``
AI / ML / DS / DL
        │
Intelligent Data Pipeline
        │
Multi-Agentic Pipeline
        │
Digital Twin
        │
        ▼
   PRODUCT VISION
        │
        ▼
     USER VALUE
```

The technology should support the Product Vision — **not define it**.

---

## 5. Product Vision Test

Read the completed Vision aloud.

The team should be able to answer:

* [ ] Is the target user clear?
* [ ] Is the problem clear?
* [ ] Is the intended product capability clear?
* [ ] Is the expected value clear?
* [ ] Can a non-technical person understand it?
* [ ] Does it describe the desired future rather than implementation details?
* [ ] Can the team use it to guide Product Backlog decisions?

If the answer to most of these is **Yes**, the Vision is ready to guide the next stage.

---

## Vision → Goals → Backlog

The Product Vision provides direction:

``
PROBLEM
   +
USERS
   +
DESIRED VALUE
       │
       ▼
PRODUCT VISION
       │
       ▼
PRODUCT GOALS
       │
       ▼
USER STORIES
       │
       ▼
PRODUCT BACKLOG
```

### Principle

> **A clear Product Vision keeps the team focused on value when technical choices, priorities and requirements begin to change.**

The Vision should remain relatively stable while the Product Backlog evolves.
