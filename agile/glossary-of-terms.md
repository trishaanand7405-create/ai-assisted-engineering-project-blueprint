# Agile Glossary of Terms

> **20 essential terms for working as an Agile product engineering team.**

This glossary defines the common vocabulary used throughout the **AI-Assisted Engineering Project Blueprint**.

---

## 01. Agile

An approach to product development based on **iterative delivery, continuous feedback, adaptation and continuous improvement**.

---

## 02. Product

The solution being created to address a **real or meaningful user problem** and deliver measurable value.

A product may be an application, data pipeline, AI system, multi-agent workflow, Digital Twin or another engineered solution.

---

## 03. Product Vision

A concise statement describing **what the product is, who it serves, what problem it addresses and why it matters**.

---

## 04. Product Goal

The longer-term outcome the team intends to achieve through development of the product.

The Product Goal provides direction for successive Sprints.

---

## 05. Product Owner

The team member accountable for **product value, priorities and the Product Backlog**.

The Product Owner helps ensure that the team builds **the right product**.

---

## 06. Scrum Master

The team member accountable for **facilitating the Agile process, maintaining Sprint discipline and helping remove impediments**.

The Scrum Master helps the team work effectively.

---

## 07. (DevOps) Engineer

The team member accountable for coordinating the **technical design, implementation, integration, testing, deployment and technical evolution** of the product.

DevOps in this blueprint does not require cloud, Docker or Kubernetes.

---

## 08. Product Backlog

The **ordered list of work** required to develop and improve the product.

It may contain Epics, User Stories, defects, technical work and enhancements.

---

## 09. Epic

A **large body of related work** that is too broad to complete as a single User Story or Sprint item.

An Epic is normally divided into smaller User Stories.

---

## 10. User Story

A short description of a requirement from the perspective of a user or stakeholder.

A common structure is:

> **As a [user], I want [capability], so that [value].**

---

## 11. Acceptance Criteria

The **conditions that must be satisfied** for a User Story or feature to be considered acceptable.

Acceptance Criteria make expectations testable and unambiguous.

---

## 12. Sprint

A **fixed, time-boxed development cycle** during which the team plans, builds, tests and demonstrates a product increment.

In this workshop, each Sprint is **1 hour**.

---

## 13. Sprint Goal

A concise statement describing **the meaningful outcome the team intends to achieve during a Sprint**.

It gives the Sprint a clear purpose.

---

## 14. Sprint Backlog

The set of Product Backlog items and associated work that the team selects and plans to accomplish during a Sprint.

The Sprint Backlog supports achievement of the Sprint Goal.

---

## 15. Increment

A **working advancement of the product** produced during a Sprint.

An increment should be usable, testable or demonstrable according to its stage of development.

---

## 16. Sprint Review

An event where the team **demonstrates the product increment, inspects the result and collects feedback**.

Feedback may change the Product Backlog.

---

## 17. Retrospective

A short team reflection focused on **how the team worked**.

The team identifies what went well, what did not go well and what it will improve in the next Sprint.

---

## 18. Definition of Done

The team's shared standard for deciding whether work is **complete, tested, demonstrable and sufficiently understood**.

> **Done means more than "the code has been written."**

---

## 19. Impediment

Anything that **blocks, delays or significantly reduces the team's ability to achieve its Sprint Goal**.

Impediments should be made visible and addressed quickly.

---

## 20. Technical Debt

The future cost created when a team chooses a **quick, incomplete or less-than-ideal technical solution** instead of addressing the underlying problem properly.

Technical debt should be made visible and managed rather than ignored.

---

# Quick Vocabulary Map

```text
PRODUCT
   │
   ├── Product Vision
   ├── Product Goal
   │
   └── Product Backlog
          │
          ├── Epic
          │     └── User Story
          │            └── Acceptance Criteria
          │
          ▼
       SPRINT
          │
          ├── Sprint Goal
          ├── Sprint Backlog
          │
          ▼
       INCREMENT
          │
          ├── Sprint Review
          │       └── Feedback
          │
          └── Retrospective
                  └── Improvement
                          │
                          ▼
                    NEXT SPRINT

Throughout the lifecycle:
Impediments → Remove / Escalate
Technical Debt → Record / Manage
Definition of Done → Quality Gate
```

## One Common Language

> **Use these terms consistently. A shared vocabulary creates shared understanding, and shared understanding enables effective teamwork.**
