# Quality Overview

> **Quality is built into the product; it is not inspected into the product at the end.**

Quality in AI-Assisted Engineering means delivering a product that satisfies its intended requirements, behaves reliably, remains understandable and can be maintained by the team.

Quality is a **shared engineering responsibility**.

---

## 1. Quality Across the Lifecycle

Quality begins before coding.

``
PROBLEM
   ↓
REQUIREMENTS
   ↓
DESIGN
   ↓
IMPLEMENTATION
   ↓
TESTING
   ↓
RELEASE
   ↓
MAINTENANCE
```

Each stage creates opportunities to prevent defects rather than discovering them later.

---

## 2. Dimensions of Quality

Teams should consider:

| Dimension       | Question                                        |
| --------------- | ----------------------------------------------- |
| Correctness     | Does it produce the intended result?            |
| Usability       | Can users use it effectively?                   |
| Reliability     | Does it behave consistently?                    |
| Performance     | Does it perform adequately?                     |
| Security        | Is it protected from avoidable risks?           |
| Maintainability | Can the team change it safely?                  |
| Testability     | Can its behaviour be validated?                 |
| Sustainability  | Does it avoid unnecessary resource consumption? |

Not every project requires the same depth in every dimension.

---

## 3. Quality and Requirements

A product cannot be considered high quality if it solves the wrong problem.

Quality therefore depends on:

* Clear requirements
* Explicit acceptance criteria
* Appropriate user expectations
* Correct implementation
* Evidence-based validation

> **Correctly building the wrong thing is still poor quality.**

---

## 4. Quality and AI

AI-assisted development does not reduce quality responsibilities.

AI-generated:

* Requirements
* Designs
* Code
* Tests
* Documentation
* Data transformations

must be reviewed and validated appropriately.

The team remains accountable for the final result.

---

## 5. Quality Evidence

Teams should be able to demonstrate quality through evidence such as:

* Passing tests
* Acceptance Criteria
* Review results
* Validation reports
* Demonstrations
* Performance observations
* Deployment verification

Claims about quality should be supported by evidence wherever practical.

---

## 6. Quality vs. Perfection

Workshop projects do not need production-scale perfection.

They need **appropriate quality for their intended purpose**.

Teams should avoid both extremes:

``
UNDER-ENGINEERING ←──── APPROPRIATE QUALITY ────→ OVER-ENGINEERING
```

The goal is not to maximise every quality attribute.

The goal is to achieve the **right level of quality for the product, users and context**.

---

## Guiding Principle

> **Build quality continuously, verify it objectively and improve it deliberately.**

Quality is not the responsibility of a separate "quality person".

**The entire engineering team owns quality.**
