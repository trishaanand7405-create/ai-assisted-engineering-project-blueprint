# Users & Personas

> **A product creates value for people. Understand the people before designing the solution.**

After defining the problem, the team identifies **who experiences it, who uses the product and who is affected by its outcomes**.

---

## 1. Identify the Users

Start by identifying the people who will interact with or benefit from the product.

Typical categories include:

* **Primary User** — directly uses the product.
* **Secondary User** — supports or interacts with the product indirectly.
* **Decision-Maker** — uses product information to make decisions.
* **Operator** — manages or monitors the product.
* **Stakeholder** — has an interest in the product outcome.

Not every project requires all categories.

---

## 2. Create a Simple Persona

A persona is a **representative description of a user type**, created to help the team understand their needs and context.

A useful persona should capture:

```text
NAME / ROLE
Who is this person?

CONTEXT
Where and when do they use the product?

GOALS
What are they trying to achieve?

PAIN POINTS
What problems do they experience?

NEEDS
What would help them?

EXPECTED VALUE
How would a successful product help them?
```

A persona does not need to represent a real individual.

> **Personas describe user types, not personal identities.**

---

## 3. Understand User Needs

For each important user, ask:

* What are they trying to accomplish?
* What information do they need?
* What decisions do they make?
* What frustrates them today?
* What would make their work easier or better?
* What would make them trust the product?

Avoid designing features simply because they are technically interesting.

---

## 4. Identify the Primary User

Every project should identify at least **one primary user**.

The team should be able to complete:

> **Our primary user is __________ who needs to __________ because __________.**

This user becomes an important reference when prioritising product requirements.

---

## 5. User-to-Value Mapping

Connect users to the problem and expected value:

``
USER
  ↓
NEED
  ↓
PROBLEM
  ↓
PRODUCT CAPABILITY
  ↓
VALUE
```

For example:

```text
Data Analyst
     ↓
Needs faster data interpretation
     ↓
Manual analysis takes too long
     ↓
Intelligent analytics pipeline
     ↓
Faster access to useful insights
```

The actual solution may differ by project track.

---

## User & Persona Quality Check

Before moving forward:

* [ ] Have we identified the primary user?
* [ ] Do we understand the user's problem?
* [ ] Are the user's goals clear?
* [ ] Have we identified important pain points?
* [ ] Can we explain the value the product should provide?
* [ ] Are our personas realistic rather than invented stereotypes?
* [ ] Are we avoiding unnecessary personal information?

### Principle

> **If we cannot clearly identify who benefits from the product, we are not yet ready to define the product.**

User understanding feeds directly into the **Product Vision, Product Goals and User Stories**.
