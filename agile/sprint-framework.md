# Sprint Framework

> **Eight Sprints. Eight hours. One continuous product journey.**

The project is delivered through **8 time-boxed Sprints**, each lasting **1 hour**.

Every Sprint follows the same Agile delivery cycle:

```text
PLAN → BUILD → TEST → REVIEW → RETROSPECT → ADAPT
```

Each Sprint should produce a **demonstrable product increment**.

---

## The 8-Sprint Journey

| Sprint | Primary Focus                 | Expected Outcome                                   |
| ------ | ----------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| **S1** | Product Vision & Discovery    | Problem, users, vision and initial scope           |
| **S2** | Product Backlog & Design      | Prioritised backlog, stories and solution approach |
| **S3** | Foundation                    | Data, knowledge, state or technical foundation     |
| **S4** | Core Intelligence             | First meaningful intelligent capability            |
| **S5** | Application Integration       | Working end-to-end product                         |
| **S6** | Deployment & Usage            | Usable product                                     |
| **S7** | Maintenance & Enhancement     | Change implemented and validated                   |
| **S8** | Release & Final Demonstration | Final product increment and executive demo         |

The exact implementation differs according to the project track.

---

## One-Hour Sprint Structure

Each Sprint is deliberately time-boxed.

### Recommended allocation

| Activity                 |       Time |
| ------------------------ | ---------: |
| Sprint Planning          |     10 min |
| Build / Implement / Test |     35 min |
| Sprint Review / Demo     |     10 min |
| Retrospective            |      5 min |
| **Total**                | **60 min** |

The team may adjust the internal allocation when necessary, but the **60-minute Sprint time-box must be respected**.

---

## Sprint Goal

Every Sprint begins with one clear statement:

> **By the end of this Sprint, our team will be able to ________.**

The Sprint Goal should describe **value or a meaningful capability**, not merely a list of tasks.

### Example

Weak:

> Complete Python preprocessing code.

Better:

> By the end of this Sprint, the team will have a validated dataset ready for model training.

---

## Sprint Backlog

The team selects the highest-priority work required to achieve the Sprint Goal.

Each item should have:

* clear ownership
* an expected outcome
* acceptance criteria where appropriate
* a realistic scope for the available time

Do not overload the Sprint.

> **A smaller completed increment is better than a large unfinished backlog.**

---

## Sprint Increment

At the end of every Sprint, the team should have something that can be:

* demonstrated
* tested
* reviewed
* evaluated
* improved in the next Sprint

The increment does **not** need to be production-ready in early Sprints.

It must, however, represent **real progress toward the Product Goal**.

---

## Sprint Review

The team demonstrates the increment and receives feedback.

The review should answer:

1. What did we build?
2. Does it work?
3. What did we learn?
4. What should change?

Feedback becomes new or updated backlog items.

---

## Sprint Retrospective

The team spends the final minutes reflecting on its way of working.

Ask:

* **What went well?**
* **What did not go well?**
* **What will we improve in the next Sprint?**

Choose at least **one actionable improvement**.

---

## The Golden Rule

> **Every Sprint must leave the product better than it was at the beginning of the Sprint.**

The objective is not to complete eight isolated assignments.

The objective is to **continuously evolve one product through eight iterations**.
