# Sprint Readiness Checklist

> **A Sprint should begin with a clear objective, achievable work and a shared understanding of what success looks like.**

Use this checklist before starting a Sprint.

---

## 1. Sprint Objective

* [ ] A clear Sprint Goal has been defined.
* [ ] The Sprint Goal supports the Product Goal.
* [ ] The team understands why the Sprint matters.
* [ ] The intended outcome is understandable to everyone.
* [ ] The Sprint Goal is achievable within the available capacity.

---

## 2. Product Backlog Readiness

* [ ] Candidate Product Backlog items are available.
* [ ] High-priority items are identified.
* [ ] Relevant User Stories are understandable.
* [ ] Acceptance Criteria are available or can be established.
* [ ] Important dependencies are visible.
* [ ] Unnecessary work has been excluded from the Sprint.

---

## 3. Work Selection

* [ ] Selected items contribute to the Sprint Goal.
* [ ] Work is appropriately sized.
* [ ] Major technical dependencies are understood.
* [ ] Required design or research work is identified.
* [ ] Testing and validation work is included where necessary.
* [ ] Documentation work is included where necessary.

---

## 4. Team Capacity

* [ ] Team availability is understood.
* [ ] Planned work reflects realistic capacity.
* [ ] Known leave or availability constraints are considered.
* [ ] External dependencies are recognised.
* [ ] The team has avoided planning beyond reasonable capacity.

---

## 5. Engineering Readiness

* [ ] Required development environments are available.
* [ ] Repository access is available.
* [ ] Required tools and dependencies are understood.
* [ ] Technical uncertainties are identified.
* [ ] Important architecture or design decisions are available.
* [ ] Required test environments or data are available.

---

## 6. AI-Assisted Work

* [ ] The team has identified appropriate opportunities for AI assistance.
* [ ] AI-generated output will be reviewed.
* [ ] Sensitive information will not be unnecessarily exposed.
* [ ] AI-assisted code will be tested.
* [ ] AI-generated assumptions will be validated.
* [ ] Human ownership of Sprint decisions is clear.

---

## 7. Quality Readiness

* [ ] Definition of Done is understood.
* [ ] Acceptance Criteria are testable.
* [ ] Testing activities are planned.
* [ ] Validation activities are planned where necessary.
* [ ] Quality risks are visible.
* [ ] Evidence required to demonstrate completion is understood.

---

## 8. Delivery Readiness

* [ ] The team understands the expected Sprint increment.
* [ ] Integration work is identified.
* [ ] Deployment implications are understood.
* [ ] Verification requirements are known.
* [ ] Potential release or demonstration needs are considered.

---

## 9. Sprint Risks

* [ ] Major risks have been discussed.
* [ ] Important assumptions are visible.
* [ ] Known blockers have owners or actions.
* [ ] External dependencies have been identified.
* [ ] The team knows how to escalate significant impediments.

---

## 10. Final Readiness Check

Before starting the Sprint, the team should be able to answer:

* [ ] What is our Sprint Goal?
* [ ] Why does it matter?
* [ ] What work will move us toward that goal?
* [ ] What does "Done" mean?
* [ ] How will we demonstrate the result?
* [ ] What could prevent us from succeeding?
* [ ] What is the first concrete action?

---

## Sprint Decision

### Ready

The team has sufficient clarity and capacity to begin.

### Not Ready

Important uncertainty, dependency or capacity issues remain.

Resolve the most important blockers before committing to the Sprint.

---

## Guiding Principle

> **Plan enough to create confidence, but keep enough flexibility to learn and adapt during the Sprint.**
